Modules 1 to 3: First-order time and frequency responses, and the second-order response

A sensor never reports the present

It reports the past, smoothed. A first-order sensor obeys:

τ·dy/dt + y = K·x(t)   ⇔   G(s) = K / (τs + 1)
Step of size A:  y(t) = K·A·(1 − e−t/τ)   |   Ramp of rate R:  y(t) = K·R·[t − τ(1 − e−t/τ)]

Module 3: Second-Order Response (accelerometer, mass-spring-damper)

A mass on a spring with a damper does not settle like a thermometer. It can overshoot and ring, or creep slowly, depending on how much damping it has.

G(s) = K·ωn² / (s² + 2ζωn·s + ωn²)   ⇔   m·x″ + b·x′ + k·x = F(t)
ωn = √(k/m)  |  ζ = b / (2√(k·m))  |  overshoot = 100·e−πζ/√(1−ζ²) %  |  peak time = π / (ωn·√(1−ζ²))
